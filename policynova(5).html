01 · The problem

A Real Problem, But Is It Worth Solving?

People don't lose money because insurance is expensive. They lose it because they make high stakes decisions without understanding what they've signed.

I had seen the same pattern repeatedly in conversations with customers, policyholders, friends and family. People were often confident they were covered for something, only to discover they were not at the worst possible moment, especially when they needed to make a claim.

Almost nobody reads the fine print. It is a rational response to documents written to be legally precise rather than humanly readable. People therefore rely on an impression of what they bought, and that impression can quietly drift away from what the policy actually says.

What struck me was that no one in the market had a strong reason to close that understanding gap. Insurer apps primarily serve the insurer. Comparison sites monetise the sale, so their job largely ends at purchase. Understanding sits between those experiences, and the cost of getting it wrong lands on the policyholder.

That made the first product question deliberately uncomfortable: the problem was real, but was it frequent, valuable and commercially meaningful enough to justify building around?

04 · Competitive landscape & market learnings

What the Market Had Already Taught Us

The gap was not a lack of insurance products. It was that most tools were optimised for buying, storing or selling cover rather than helping people interpret what they already had. I then looked at products that had tried the clarity-first model to understand what the market had already learned.

UK · 2016–2020

Brolly

Closest precedent to PolicyNova

Brolly combined an insurance wallet with policy analysis, gap detection and market shopping. It was well funded and partnered with major insurers, yet later moved into selling its own insurance before being acquired by Direct Line Group and closing the consumer app.

Market learning

Clarity solved a genuine user problem, but the business eventually moved toward distribution. Utility alone did not create a strong enough revenue engine.

Germany · 2015 → present

Clark

Insurance wallet at scale

Clark lets customers bring policies into one place, analyse their cover and compare alternatives. It scaled across European markets, but does so as a licensed broker rather than as a paid clarity tool.

Market learning

The wallet attracts and serves the user; insurer commission from distribution funds the model. Understanding is valuable, but it is not the primary revenue source.

Singapore · 2025 → present

Forgettable

A newer clarity-first play

Forgettable brings scattered policies into one dashboard, explains cover in everyday language and flags gaps, overlaps and renewals. Its published direction also moves toward helping users purchase protection.

Market learning

Even a newer interpretation-first product is building toward a purchase path. The same commercial tension is still present.

05 · The defining trade off

Helpful Product or Regulated Advice?

The regulatory analysis shaped the product architecture. I compared three routes and chose the lowest risk option for the MVP so PolicyNova could test comprehension and trust without drifting into regulated insurance distribution.

Why I chose Option A for MVP

Trade offUsers leave PolicyNova when they want to purchase, so the switching journey is less seamless.
BenefitThe MVP remains an information product while we test whether users trust the interpretation layer.
ControlsNo advice language, no pre filled purchase flows, clear disclaimers, jurisdiction gating and an audit trail.
Product decisionEarn trust first. Distribution can be sequenced later once the core behaviour is proven.

Option B · Future route

Useful once the core product is trusted and partner responsibility is contractually clear.

Option C · Later stage

Full distribution offers the strongest journey control, but only after validation justifies the regulatory and operational burden.

07 · The product

Turning the Insight Into a Testable Product

The key journey moves from policy ingestion to plain English interpretation, decision support and timely prompts.

Step 01 · Onboarding

Context before content

A three step signup captures who the user is and, critically, the focus areas they care about, so the AI can summarise around their pain points rather than a generic template.

PM decision: personalisation starts before the first upload. Asking for focus areas up front makes every later summary feel built for them.
policynova.co.uk/signup/step1
Step 02 · Tailoring

Only the questions that matter

Users select the insurance types they hold. Multi select, skippable, low friction; the onboarding never blocks the path to value.

PM decision: "Skip for now" on every optional step. In a low engagement category, forcing completeness kills activation.
policynova.co.uk/signup/step2
Step 03 · The wait, reframed

Turning dead time into education

Analysis takes 30–60 seconds. Instead of a blank spinner, the wait surfaces rotating "Did you know?" cards busting real insurance myths: claims based renewal pricing, cooling off fees, no claims discount impact.

PM decision: the loading state is a product surface, not a gap. Every myth shown is one the complaints desk taught me people learn too late.
policynova.co.uk/document-upload
Step 04 · The plain English summary

Covered and excluded, at a glance

The core payoff: a two column breakdown of exactly what's covered and what's excluded, plus a step by step claim process. The exact moment the complaints desk problem gets solved upstream.

PM decision: lead with exclusions, not just cover. "Claims below £250 excess" and "wear and tear" are precisely what people discover too late.
policynova.co.uk/policy/1
Step 05 · Market comparison

Above or below market, factually

A neutral bar chart puts the user's premium against the market average, with a plain statement of the gap: "You're paying 12% above similar policies." No "switch now" button at MVP, by design.

PM decision: this is where the regulatory trade off becomes visible. At MVP we state the fact and stop, which keeps us outside regulated distribution while trust is being built. The buy flow is a later release,…
policynova.co.uk/policy/1
Step 06 · Duplicate detection

Finding money the user didn't know they were losing

PolicyAI flags overlapping cover with evidence (here, duplicate protection across an Aviva home policy and a British Gas boiler plan) and routes to the next steps.

PM decision: proactive, evidence backed flags build trust faster than any marketing. The product does work the user didn't ask for, and finds real savings.
policynova.co.uk/duplicates
Step 07 · Ask anything

PolicyAI, grounded in your actual policies

A conversational assistant answers real questions ("Do I have duplicate cover?", "Am I covered for all home damages?", "How do I claim on my car?") grounded in the user's own documents.

PM decision: seed the empty state with the three questions people most need answered. The blank chat box is where most AI products lose the user.
policynova.co.uk/policyai

09 · Reflection

A Real Problem Alone Is Not a Scalable Business

PolicyNova changed the question from “can this problem be solved?” to “is this problem broad, frequent and commercially strong enough to support a durable product?” The evidence suggested that clarity has value, but clarity alone may be too episodic to sustain the original business model.

The opportunity may need to be bigger.

If PolicyNova were taken further, I would test a broader insurance decision platform and distribution model rather than assume policy interpretation alone can scale. The precedent work suggests the commercial engine may eventually sit closer to insurance distribution.

Regulatory constraints are design inputs, not blockers.

Working through FCA and IDD boundaries changed the architecture itself. Regulation helped define what the MVP should do, what language it should avoid and what should be sequenced later.

Restraint is strategy.

The highest leverage decision was not another feature. It was deciding not to sell policies, submit claims or provide personalised advice before the core comprehension behaviour had been proven.
